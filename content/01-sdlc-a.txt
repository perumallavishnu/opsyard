In the early days teams simply wrote code and repaired it until it seemed to work. This is **code-and-fix**. For a tiny program it is fine.
For anything larger nobody knows what is finished, what is correct, or what it will cost.
= The software crisis | PROBLEM
" c It is two years late and over budget.
" d Nobody wrote down what you wanted.
: s err cancelled
By the late 1960s programs had grown faster than the ways to manage them. Projects ran late, over budget, failed, or were never used. A NATO conference in 1968 in Garmisch, Germany, discussed this as the **software crisis** and made the term **software engineering** popular.

Because the steps are known, work can be shared by hundreds of people, in several countries, over many years. Banks, aircraft, hospitals and phones depend on this. Costs can be estimated, quality audited, and new staff trained.

# sdlc-history | A short history of software process | Beginner | From code-and-fix to DevOps.
@h 1968 Software crisis ; 1970 Waterfall ; 1986 Spiral ; 1995 Scrum ; 2001 Agile Manifesto ; 2009 DevOps Days ; 2020 Scrum Guide
= 1968: a name for the problem | HISTORY
The NATO conference at Garmisch brings the words "software engineering" into common use: build software with the discipline of other engineering.
= 1970: the waterfall paper | HISTORY
Winston Royce describes large projects as a series of phases. The single pass he drew is remembered as **Waterfall**, although he warned it was risky and advised iterating.
= 1986: risk first | HISTORY
Barry Boehm presents the **Spiral model**: short rounds, each reducing the biggest risk. Iterative ideas spread.
= 1995: Scrum | HISTORY
Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland present **Scrum**. Around the same years Extreme Programming (XP) appears.
= 2001: the Agile Manifesto | HISTORY
Seventeen practitioners meet in Snowbird, Utah, and agree on four values and twelve principles.
= 2009: DevOps | HISTORY
Patrick Debois organises the first **DevOps Days** in Ghent, Belgium. Development and operations start to work as one team.
= Today | HISTORY
The current Scrum Guide dates from November 2020. Most teams mix: Scrum or Kanban for planning, DevOps automation for delivery, and stricter models where safety law asks for them.
